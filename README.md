THE D9 MODEL

A Conceptual Framework for Planet-Assessment-Driven Biological Seeding

Concept report • Version 1.0 • 18 October 2026

Abstract

The D9 Model is an early-stage conceptual framework exploring whether biological systems could, over long timescales, contribute to the development of a planetary environment toward greater biological complexity. It is not restricted to a single target such as the Moon. Instead, it proposes a planet-first approach: assess a reachable celestial body's environmental conditions, then determine whether a suitable biological strategy could be considered. The long-term vision begins with microorganisms capable of oxygen-producing photosynthesis under appropriate conditions, followed by consideration of atmospheric ozone formation, additional microorganisms, and eventually plants or more complex biological systems. These stages are a proposed research sequence, not a demonstrated or guaranteed outcome.

1. Project Overview

Project name: D9 Model
Research areas: Astrobiology, planetary science, biotechnology, computational biology, and space systems.
Project status: Conceptual proposal; feasibility and implementation have not been experimentally demonstrated.

The D9 Model considers a broad question: could a carefully assessed and appropriately selected planetary environment ever support a staged biological-seeding concept? The model does not assume that humans must be the eventual beneficiaries. Its long-term vision leaves open the possibility of naturally developing biological complexity, while recognizing that the emergence of a civilization is highly speculative and cannot be predicted from microbial seeding alone.

2. Core Principle: Planet-First Assessment

The target is not fixed in advance. The proposed process is to study any reachable celestial body first and evaluate its conditions before deciding whether the D9 concept is appropriate. A target-specific plan would require evidence about the environment and careful consideration of scientific, technical, ethical, and planetary-protection constraints.

2.1 Candidate assessment factors

Atmospheric composition and availability of relevant gases.

Temperature range and environmental variability.

Availability and state of water, including possible ice or liquid water.

Radiation exposure.

Surface materials, minerals, and potential nutrient availability.

Gravity and atmospheric pressure.

Possibility of indigenous life, or of biological contamination from previous missions.

These are proposed factors for a future assessment framework. The current concept does not yet specify a validated scoring system, data pipeline, thresholds, or decision algorithm.

3. Proposed Biological Development Sequence

Stage 1 — Initial microorganisms and oxygen production

The concept proposes considering microorganisms, including cyanobacteria, for an initial biological stage. Cyanobacteria can release oxygen through oxygenic photosynthesis in suitable environments. Whether this could work on a particular celestial body depends on factors such as light, temperature, pressure, water availability, chemistry, and radiation; it is not established for any target by this report.

Stage 2 — Possible ozone formation

The long-term concept then considers whether atmospheric oxygen could contribute to ozone formation. Ozone production and the development of a persistent protective ozone layer require appropriate atmospheric chemistry and conditions. Oxygen production by itself does not guarantee an ozone layer.

Stage 3 — Additional microorganisms

The project considers the possibility of introducing additional microbial groups, including purple bacteria, if future research establishes that the target environment is suitable. Their role and suitability remain to be defined; different groups have different metabolic requirements and do not all produce oxygen.

Stage 4 — Plants and more complex biological systems

Plants and more complex biological systems are a later hypothetical stage. Their feasibility would depend on environmental conditions and the establishment of many necessary biological and chemical cycles. The report does not claim that this progression would necessarily occur.

Long-term vision — Biological complexity and possible civilization

The inventor's broader vision is to explore whether biological development could, over very long timescales, contribute to a more complex ecosystem and potentially a civilization. This is speculative and is not a direct or predictable consequence of introducing microorganisms.

4. Scientific Background

4.1 Oparin–Haldane hypothesis

The Oparin–Haldane hypothesis is a historical framework for chemical evolution, proposing that organic molecules and increasingly complex chemistry could have developed under conditions on early Earth before the emergence of life. It provides background for questions about life's origins; it does not itself demonstrate that life can be created on another planet.

4.2 Miller–Urey experiment

The Miller–Urey experiment demonstrated that organic compounds, including amino acids, could form from simpler chemicals under a particular simulated early-Earth environment with an energy source. It did not create living organisms or demonstrate planetary oxygenation. In the D9 report, it is relevant as historical background for prebiotic chemistry, not as direct evidence that the proposed seeding sequence will work.

4.3 Oxygen-producing microorganisms

Cyanobacteria are known for oxygenic photosynthesis on Earth. A proposed use beyond Earth would require separate assessment of the target environment, organism survival, resource availability, ecological effects, and planetary-protection requirements. This report does not specify or validate genetic modifications.

5. Computational Biology and Modelling: Proposed Future Work

The original concept refers to computational biology and planetary adaptation, but the implementation details are not yet defined. No validated simulation, target-selection algorithm, biological model, or experimental dataset has been supplied for this version of the report. These are therefore identified as future work rather than described as completed capabilities.

Define the environmental variables and reliable data sources required for candidate assessment.

Establish a transparent method for comparing environmental constraints without implying that one score proves habitability.

Specify what the model predicts, what evidence supports each assumption, and how uncertainty will be reported.

Separate simulated outputs from measured observations and experimental results.

Review planetary-protection and contamination constraints before considering any biological deployment.

6. Planetary Protection, Ethics, and Feasibility

Deliberately introducing terrestrial organisms to another celestial body raises substantial scientific and ethical questions. A target that appears uninhabited may still have unknown or poorly understood environments relevant to the search for indigenous life. Contamination could compromise scientific investigations and could have irreversible consequences. No claim of legal compliance or zero contamination risk is made here. Any real mission would require independent review against applicable international obligations, mission-specific rules, and current planetary-protection guidance.

The earlier idea of using rocket-engine heat to melt ice is not treated as a required part of this version. The project lead has indicated that this was a tentative idea and that liquid water may or may not be necessary. The D9 mechanism must remain open until target requirements and feasibility are assessed.

7. Current Status and Limitations

The D9 Model is currently a conceptual proposal.

The target celestial body has not been selected.

The biological community and its environmental requirements have not been finalized.

The role of liquid water, energy sources, atmospheric conditions, and containment is unresolved.

No verified calculations, validated code, laboratory results, or field results were provided for this report version.

A staged progression from microorganisms to an ozone layer, plants, or civilization is not established and may be infeasible on many bodies.

8. Proposed Research Roadmap

Review current planetary science and astrobiology literature and identify which assumptions have empirical support.

Define the intended meaning of 'biological seeding' and the measurable objective of each proposed stage.

Build a target-assessment framework using traceable environmental data and explicit uncertainty.

Determine whether the model is intended to be a qualitative decision framework, a computational simulation, or both.

Evaluate planetary-protection and ethical constraints before considering any organism introduction.

Develop and validate any computational component before making performance claims.

Publish limitations and negative results as clearly as positive results.

9. Conclusion

The D9 Model proposes a planet-first framework for exploring staged biological seeding, beginning with an assessment of environmental conditions and considering oxygen-producing microorganisms as a possible initial stage. It then hypothesizes later possibilities involving ozone formation, other microorganisms, plants, and greater biological complexity. Its value at this stage is as a research question connecting astrobiology, computational biology, biotechnology, and planetary science. Substantial scientific, technical, ethical, and planetary-protection questions remain unresolved. The report therefore presents D9 as a concept for critical evaluation and future research, not as a proven or deployment-ready technology.
